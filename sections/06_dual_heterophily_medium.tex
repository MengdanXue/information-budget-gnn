\section{Two-Hop Recovery as a Heterophily Diagnostic}
\label{sec:heterophily}

Low-homophily datasets exhibit inconsistent graph-model outcomes. We report two-hop label agreement as one descriptive view of longer-range structure, not as a model-selection variable.

\subsection{The Heterophily Puzzle}

Traditional wisdom suggests GNNs fail on heterophilic graphs because neighbors have different labels. However, this oversimplifies:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Texas} ($h = 0.087$): MLP achieves 80.5\%, GCN achieves 56.3\% (GCN harms)
    \item \textbf{Chameleon} ($h = 0.231$): MLP achieves 51.4\%, while the best recorded graph model, H2GCN, achieves 65.5\%
\end{itemize}

Both have similar homophily, yet opposite outcomes. Low homophily alone cannot explain this.

\subsection{Two-Hop Quantities}

\begin{definition}[2-Hop Homophily]
\label{def:2hop}
Let $h_1$ denote one-hop label agreement and $h_2$ denote two-hop label agreement:
\begin{equation}
    h_2 = \mathbb{P}(Y_v = Y_u \mid \text{dist}(u, v) = 2)
\end{equation}
We report the absolute change and, secondarily, the ratio
\begin{align}
    \Delta h &= h_2-h_1, \\
    R &= \frac{h_2}{h_1}.
\end{align}
For empirical class proportions $\widehat p_c$, the class-prior agreement baseline is
\begin{equation}
    h_0=\sum_c \widehat p_c^2.
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

The absolute change $\Delta h$ remains on the probability scale. By contrast, $R$ is unstable when $h_1$ is near zero and can look large even when $h_2$ is modest. Neither quantity measures improvement relative to chance unless it is also compared with $h_0$.

\textbf{Evidence availability}: The audited 10-seed result retains point estimates of $h_1$ and $h_2$ and paired model accuracies. It does not retain class counts or resampled one-hop/two-hop pairs. Consequently, the empirical class-prior baseline $h_0$ and uncertainty intervals for the structural point estimates are \emph{not archived}; they are shown as unavailable rather than reconstructed from an assumed class balance. Performance uncertainty is reported separately below.

\subsection{H2GCN Performance Analysis}

H2GCN \cite{zhu2020beyond} is designed for heterophilic graphs, using:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Ego-neighbor separation (preserve original features)
    \item Higher-order neighborhoods (2-hop aggregation)
    \item Combination of intermediate representations
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Protocol-specific performance result}: Across 10 paired seeds, H2GCN--MLP mean differences (95\% paired bootstrap intervals) are +6.3 points [1.3, 11.1] on Texas, $-0.8$ [$-4.3$, 2.5] on Wisconsin, +1.3 [$-2.6$, 5.0] on Cornell, +14.1 [12.2, 15.9] on Chameleon, and +10.0 [8.1, 11.8] on Squirrel. Thus positive $\Delta h$ does not imply that H2GCN clearly exceeds MLP, and non-positive $\Delta h$ does not imply that it fails to do so.

The architecture comparison is also not isolated to two-hop aggregation: H2GCN includes ego-neighbor separation and combines intermediate representations. The paired results therefore evaluate the complete trained model, not the causal effect of the two-hop channel alone.

\subsection{What the Statistic Does Not Establish}

The sign of $\Delta h$ does not identify an edge-formation mechanism, feature utility, or the best architecture. In particular, similar values can arise from different class-transition matrices, and the global statistic discards which class pairs contribute to agreement. Claims about class-independent edge formation require a separate generative or causal analysis.

\subsection{Descriptive Reporting Checklist}

\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=green!5!white,colframe=green!75!black,title=Two-Hop Diagnostic Report]
For every dataset:
\begin{itemize}
    \item report $h_0$, $h_1$, $h_2$, and $\Delta h$ on the probability scale;
    \item if $R$ is included, show $h_1$ beside it and warn about near-zero instability;
    \item report sampling or bootstrap uncertainty when the required pairs are retained, otherwise mark it unavailable; and
    \item compare candidate models on matched splits and seeds without mapping a cutoff to a winning family.
\end{itemize}
\end{tcolorbox}

The archived results do not support fixed ``strong'' or ``absent'' recovery thresholds for automatic selection.
