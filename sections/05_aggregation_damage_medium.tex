\section{Aggregation Damage Analysis}
\label{sec:adr}

We compare graph models with a feature-only reference using a signed accuracy difference. This empirical quantity is separate from the moment ratio $\kappa$ and does not identify why two trained models differ.

\subsection{Aggregation Difference Relative to MLP}

\begin{definition}[Aggregation Difference Relative to MLP]
For estimates obtained on the same evaluation protocol, we retain the acronym ADR and define the absolute difference
\begin{equation}
    \text{ADR} = 100\bigl(\widehat{\mathrm{Acc}}_{\mathrm{MLP}}
    - \widehat{\mathrm{Acc}}_{\mathrm{GNN}}\bigr)
\end{equation}
in percentage points. ADR $>0$ means the MLP has higher recorded accuracy; ADR $<0$ means the graph model has higher recorded accuracy. Neither sign alone attributes the difference to aggregation, because architecture, tuning, optimization, and sampling also differ.
\end{definition}

In the controlled sweep summarized below, ADR is largest near $h=0.5$ for GCN. The fixed-degree neighbor-only calculation also has zero class-mean separation at $\rho=2h-1=0$, but this correspondence is mechanistic context rather than a causal decomposition of trained-model accuracy.

\textbf{Descriptive result} (Table~\ref{tab:adr}): In this controlled configuration, the MLP--GraphSAGE difference is 0.1 percentage points, compared with 18.9 points for GCN. We report differences rather than ratios because ratio summaries can be unstable and obscure the accuracy scale.

\subsection{Why Concatenation Helps}

\begin{theorem}[Information Preservation]
\label{thm:concat}
For concatenated representation $Z = (X, \tilde{X})$ where $\tilde{X}$ is the aggregated representation:
\begin{equation}
    I(Y; X, \tilde{X}) \geq I(Y; X)
\end{equation}
Equality holds only if $\tilde{X}$ provides no additional information given $X$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By the chain rule of mutual information:
\begin{equation}
    I(Y; X, \tilde{X}) = I(Y; X) + I(Y; \tilde{X} | X) \geq I(Y; X)
\end{equation}
since mutual information is non-negative.
\end{proof}

\textbf{Practical Implication}: The uncompressed joint representation $(X,\tilde{X})$ does not discard information relative to $X$ before learning. This is a representation-level statement; a finite trained architecture can still lose accuracy through projection, regularization, optimization, or limited data.

\subsection{Replace vs.\ Concat: Theoretical Analysis}

Consider the aggregated representation under GCN (replace) vs.\ GraphSAGE (concat):
\begin{align}
    Z_{\text{GCN}} &= \sigma(W \cdot \tilde{X}) \quad &\text{(replace)} \\
Z_{\text{SAGE}} &= \sigma\!\left(W[X\,\Vert\,\tilde{X}]\right) \quad &\text{(concat then project)}
\end{align}

For GCN, replacing $X$ with $\tilde{X}$ can discard feature information before classification. A concatenation architecture exposes both inputs to the learned projection and can in principle ignore the aggregated coordinates, but successful preservation is not guaranteed after finite-dimensional projection and training.

\subsection{Reporting and Model Comparison}

\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=blue!5!white,colframe=blue!75!black,title=Matched Comparison Checklist]
For each candidate architecture:
\begin{itemize}
    \item train the MLP and graph model on matched splits and seeds;
    \item report ADR in percentage points with paired uncertainty; and
    \item describe the architecture and tuning differences before attributing a gap to aggregation.
\end{itemize}
\end{tcolorbox}
