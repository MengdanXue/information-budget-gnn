\section{Aggregation Damage Analysis}
\label{sec:adr}

When aggregation \textit{harms} classification ($\kappa < 1$), we need metrics to quantify the damage and identify protective architectures.

\subsection{Aggregation Damage Ratio}

\begin{definition}[Aggregation Damage Ratio]
For a GNN model, we define:
\begin{equation}
    \text{ADR} = \text{Acc}_{\text{MLP}} - \text{Acc}_{\text{GNN}}
\end{equation}
ADR $> 0$ indicates aggregation destroys information; ADR $< 0$ indicates enhancement.
\end{definition}

\begin{observation}[ADR Peaks at Mid-Homophily]
ADR empirically follows an inverted U-shape, peaking near $h = 0.5$, where the fixed-degree discriminability ratio is zero because $\rho = 2h-1 = 0$.
\end{observation}

\centering
\caption{ADR by Model Type at $h=0.5$ (CSBM Synthetic)}
\label{tab:adr}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lcc@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Model} & \textbf{Aggregation} & \textbf{Accuracy loss} \\
\midrule
GCN & Replace & 18.9 points \\
GAT & Replace + Attn & 5.3 points \\
GraphSAGE & Concat & 0.1 points \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

\textbf{Key Finding} (Table~\ref{tab:adr}): In this controlled configuration, GraphSAGE reduces the observed loss relative to MLP from 18.9 percentage points for GCN to 0.1 points. We report absolute differences because ratios with a near-zero denominator are unstable and exaggerate the comparison.

\subsection{Why Concatenation Helps}

\begin{theorem}[Information Preservation]
\label{thm:concat}
For concatenated representation $Z = (X, \tilde{X})$ where $\tilde{X}$ is the aggregated representation:
\begin{equation}
    I(Y; X, \tilde{X}) \geq I(Y; X)
\end{equation}
Equality holds only if $\tilde{X}$ provides no additional information given $X$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By the chain rule of mutual information:
\begin{equation}
    I(Y; X, \tilde{X}) = I(Y; X) + I(Y; \tilde{X} | X) \geq I(Y; X)
\end{equation}
since mutual information is non-negative.
\end{proof}

\textbf{Practical Implication}: The uncompressed joint representation $(X,\tilde{X})$ does not discard information relative to $X$ before learning. This is a representation-level statement; a finite trained architecture can still lose accuracy through projection, regularization, optimization, or limited data.

\subsection{Replace vs.\ Concat: Theoretical Analysis}

Consider the aggregated representation under GCN (replace) vs.\ GraphSAGE (concat):
\begin{align}
    Z_{\text{GCN}} &= \sigma(W \cdot \tilde{X}) \quad &\text{(replace)} \\
Z_{\text{SAGE}} &= \sigma\!\left(W[X\,\Vert\,\tilde{X}]\right) \quad &\text{(concat then project)}
\end{align}

For GCN, replacing $X$ with $\tilde{X}$ can discard feature information before classification. A concatenation architecture exposes both inputs to the learned projection and can in principle ignore the aggregated coordinates, but successful preservation is not guaranteed after finite-dimensional projection and training.

\subsection{Practical Recommendation}

\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=blue!5!white,colframe=blue!75!black,title=Architecture Selection Guideline]
When homophily is uncertain ($0.3 < h < 0.7$), prefer \textbf{concatenation-based architectures} to minimize aggregation damage risk:
\begin{itemize}
    \item GraphSAGE with mean aggregation
    \item GCN with skip connections
    \item APPNP with initial residual
\end{itemize}
\end{tcolorbox}
