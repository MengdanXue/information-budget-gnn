\section{Accuracy Headroom Is Not a Structural Predictor}
\label{sec:budget}

Accuracy imposes an immediate ceiling on positive improvement, but that ceiling contains no model of graph utility. Let
\begin{equation}
    \widehat{\Delta}
    = \widehat{\mathrm{Acc}}_{\mathrm{GNN}}
    - \widehat{\mathrm{Acc}}_{\mathrm{MLP}}
\end{equation}
denote the observed accuracy difference under one fixed data split, tuning protocol, and model set. Because accuracy is at most one,
\begin{equation}
    \max(\widehat{\Delta},0)
    \leq 1-\widehat{\mathrm{Acc}}_{\mathrm{MLP}}.
    \label{eq:positive_headroom}
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:positive_headroom} is an arithmetic range statement. It does not require assumptions about model capacity, aggregation, or conditional graph information, and observing that experiments satisfy it is not an empirical validation.

The one-sided form is essential. Positive headroom places no lower bound on $\widehat{\Delta}$ and therefore cannot bound aggregation damage. For example, an MLP accuracy of $0.9$ leaves positive headroom $0.1$, but a graph-model accuracy of $0$ gives $\widehat{\Delta}=-0.9$. Similarly, the ratio $\widehat{\Delta}/(1-\widehat{\mathrm{Acc}}_{\mathrm{MLP}})$ is at most one when its denominator is positive, but it need not be greater than or equal to $-1$. We therefore do not interpret this ratio as a bounded efficiency quantity.

The notation also needs an operational distinction. The observed $\widehat{\mathrm{Acc}}_{\mathrm{MLP}}$ is a protocol-specific estimate from a tuned model and finite split; it is not the unknown best achievable feature-only accuracy. Throughout the empirical sections, ``MLP'' refers only to the declared architecture, search space, splits, and seeds. Claims about all feature-only classifiers would require a different estimand.

\subsection{Why Raw Headroom Correlations Are Misleading}

Let $H=1-\widehat{\mathrm{Acc}}_{\mathrm{MLP}}$ and $\widehat{\Delta}=\widehat{\mathrm{Acc}}_{\mathrm{GNN}}-\widehat{\mathrm{Acc}}_{\mathrm{MLP}}$. Both quantities contain the shared term $-\widehat{\mathrm{Acc}}_{\mathrm{MLP}}$, so their raw correlation can be positive even when GNN accuracy is unrelated to MLP accuracy. This mathematical coupling must be audited before a correlation is interpreted as predictive evidence.

On the nine traceable external datasets, the observed headroom--advantage correlation is $r=-0.255$ ($R^2=0.065$; 95\% bootstrap interval $[-0.743,0.523]$). A null calculation that permutes GNN accuracies while retaining the shared MLP term gives mean $r=0.669$ with a 95\% interval $[0.327,0.897]$ (Table~\ref{tab:predictive_comparison}). The previously reported large positive correlation is therefore not reproduced by the audited external result set and is not used as evidence.

\subsection{Permitted Empirical Use}

We retain headroom for two limited purposes. First, it reports the maximum possible positive accuracy gain on the chosen scale. Second, it identifies ceiling effects when comparing improvements across datasets. It is not treated as conditional mutual information, a calibrated prediction of graph utility, or evidence that a graph model cannot underperform severely. Whether a diagnostic adds value must instead be tested against operational baselines using a fixed target, matched tie handling, regret, and coverage.
