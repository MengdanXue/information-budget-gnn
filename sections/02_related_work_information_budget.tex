\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

Our analysis relates to four research areas: GNN aggregation mechanisms, homophily and heterophily, feature--structure relationships, and representation-level theory.

\subsection{Graph Neural Networks and Aggregation}

\textbf{Spectral methods}: GCN~\cite{kipf2017gcn} uses a self-looped, symmetrically normalized adjacency operator followed by learned transformations. SGC~\cite{wu2019sgc} removes intermediate nonlinearities and collapses repeated propagation. Neither operator is identical to the fixed-degree neighbor-only average analyzed in Section~\ref{sec:snr}; the simplified operator is used to isolate one mechanism.

\textbf{Spatial methods}: GraphSAGE~\cite{hamilton2017graphsage} can concatenate an ego representation with an aggregated neighborhood representation, while GIN~\cite{xu2019gin} studies expressive aggregation. Retaining an explicit ego channel can prevent forced information loss at the representation level, but this observation alone does not guarantee better finite-sample accuracy after optimization.

\textbf{Attention mechanisms}: GAT~\cite{velivckovic2018gat} and GATv2~\cite{brody2022gatv2} learn data-dependent neighbor weights. Recent CSBM analysis shows that the benefit of attention can depend on the balance between feature and structural noise~\cite{pmlr-v267-ma25w}. Results for an unweighted mean operator therefore cannot be transferred to attention without a separate argument.

\textbf{Scalability}: GraphSAINT~\cite{zeng2020graphsaint} and Cluster-GCN~\cite{chiang2019clustergcn} enable training on large graphs. Our current OGB evidence is limited to a three-seed ogbn-arxiv result and is reported descriptively; it does not establish million-node or cross-OGB validation.

\subsection{Homophily and Heterophily}

\textbf{The homophily assumption}: Classic GNNs assume connected nodes share labels. Edge homophily $h = \frac{|\{(u,v) \in E : y_u = y_v\}|}{|E|}$ measures this tendency~\cite{mcpherson2001homophily}.

\textbf{Heterophily-aware methods}: When $h$ is low, standard GNN performance is inconsistent. H2GCN~\cite{zhu2020beyond} uses 2-hop neighbors and ego-neighbor separation. LINKX~\cite{luan2022linkx} separates features from structure entirely. MixHop~\cite{abu2019mixhop} and GPR-GNN~\cite{chien2021gprgnn} use multi-hop aggregation with learnable coefficients. FAGCN~\cite{fagcn2021} introduces frequency-adaptive filtering to handle both homophily and heterophily. GloGNN~\cite{li2022glognn} discovers global homophily patterns even in locally heterophilous graphs. ACM-GCN~\cite{acmgcn2024} uses adaptive cross-hop message passing. Our recovery-ratio analysis (Section~\ref{sec:heterophily}) diagnoses whether label homophily increases at two hops without claiming that this statistic alone selects the optimal model.

\textbf{Beyond binary homophily}: Recent work challenges the simple high/low dichotomy. Platonov et al.~\cite{platonov2023heterophily} show that homophily alone is insufficient for predicting GNN success. Ma et al.~\cite{ma2021homophily} introduce class-conditional homophily. Under a heterophilous SBM, Wang et al.~\cite{pmlr-v235-wang24u} relate separability gains to distances between neighborhood distributions and average degree, rather than to one global homophily scalar. Our fixed-degree calculation complements this line by retaining edge correlation, degree, and feature strength, while our empirical audit asks whether a two-hop statistic adds decision value.

\subsection{Feature-Structure Relationships}

\textbf{When features are strong}: Huang et al.~\cite{huang2021mlp} showed that MLPs can match GNNs on some benchmarks, while GAMLP~\cite{zhang2021gamlp} combines precomputed propagation with an MLP. High observed MLP accuracy leaves little arithmetic room for positive accuracy gain, but it does not determine whether aggregation will help or cause a large negative gap.

\textbf{Feature quality metrics}: Node feature informativeness and evaluation pitfalls have been studied~\cite{shchur2018pitfalls}. We use the Gaussian-model quantity $s=\mu^\top\Sigma^{-1}\mu$ as a controlled discriminability parameter and keep observed MLP accuracy as a protocol-specific empirical baseline.

\textbf{Feature and structural noise}: Dong and Kluger~\cite{pmlr-v202-dong23a} study structural noise through an edge signal-to-noise ratio, while Ma et al.~\cite{pmlr-v267-ma25w} analyze when attention helps under different feature--structure noise regimes. These works reinforce that architecture-specific conclusions require an explicit noise model; our result instead isolates a prescribed linear center--neighbor mixture.

\textbf{Over-smoothing}: Li et al.~\cite{li2018oversmoothing} identified representation collapse in deep GNNs, and Chen et al.~\cite{chen2020oversmoothing} analyzed a spectral perspective. Our accuracy-difference diagnostic is descriptive and should not be conflated with a proof of over-smoothing or information destruction.

\subsection{GNN Expressiveness Theory}

\textbf{WL hierarchy}: Xu et al.~\cite{xu2019gin} relate message-passing expressiveness to 1-WL, and Morris et al.~\cite{morris2019wl} study higher-order variants. These results concern representational distinguishability rather than the class-conditional moment calculation studied here.

\textbf{Information and depth}: Wu et al.~\cite{wu2023infognn} analyze information flow in GNNs, while Oono and Suzuki~\cite{oono2020exponential} study asymptotic loss of expressive power with depth. We do not derive an information-theoretic bound on classification-error improvement; our exact result is a one-hop Mahalanobis-moment ratio under explicit distributional assumptions.

\textbf{CSBM thresholds and finite-depth embeddings}: Lu and Sen~\cite{lu2023contextual} characterize sharp detection and weak-recovery thresholds for the contextual stochastic block model. Dalle and Thiran~\cite{pmlr-v269-dalle25a} show that finite-depth linearized GCN embeddings on a CSBM form Gaussian mixtures and that a single-Gaussian approximation can be insufficient. Those results concern different models and objectives from our conditional fixed-degree moment calculation; we use them to delimit, not extend, the present claim.

\textbf{Aggregation analysis}: Cai and Wang~\cite{cai2020note} study limitations of mean aggregation, and Hamilton~\cite{hamilton2020book} surveys aggregator choices. The mutual-information chain rule for concatenated representations shows only that an optimal observer cannot lose information when an ego channel is retained; it does not prove that a trained GraphSAGE model must outperform GCN.

\subsection{Positioning of Our Work}

Our positioning differs from stronger predictive or threshold claims in three ways:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Restricted exact calculation}: We keep label-mixture covariance in a fixed-degree binary Gaussian model and state where the analyzed operator differs from practical GNN layers.

    \item \textbf{Direct validation standard}: We distinguish tests of the derived moment ratio from downstream accuracy experiments and do not use arithmetic non-violations as empirical confirmation.

    \item \textbf{Negative diagnostic evaluation}: We test aggregation damage and two-hop recovery against operational baselines and retain their failures rather than presenting them as universal selectors.
\end{enumerate}

Together, these choices support a scoped mechanism study and define the evidence still required for any future model-selection claim.
