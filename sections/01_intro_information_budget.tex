\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

\IEEEPARstart{G}{raph} Neural Networks (GNNs) combine node features with neighborhood information through message passing~\cite{kipf2017gcn,velivckovic2018gat,hamilton2017graphsage}. This operation can improve node classification when neighbors supply useful class information, but it can also dilute feature signal and underperform a feature-only multilayer perceptron (MLP). We study the mechanism behind this variation and ask a deliberately limited question: \textbf{when does a controlled aggregation operator improve class-conditional feature discriminability, and how far can simple graph statistics diagnose the same effect on real data?}

Edge homophily alone cannot answer this question. Homophily records the fraction of same-label edges, whereas aggregation also depends on neighborhood size, feature strength, label-mixture variance, normalization, and retention of the center node's features. Consequently, graphs with similar homophily can exhibit different GNN--MLP gaps, and heterophilic graphs with similar one-hop statistics can favor different architectures~\cite{zhu2020beyond}. A scalar structural statistic may therefore describe one mechanism without being a reliable model selector.

We first isolate the effect of aggregation in a restricted probabilistic model. Nodes have binary labels and Gaussian class-conditional features; every center node has fixed degree $d$; and neighbor labels are conditionally independent with edge-label correlation $\rho$. For neighbor-only averaging, the exact ratio between aggregated and self-feature squared-Mahalanobis discriminability is
\begin{equation}
    \kappa=\frac{\rho^2d}{1+(1-\rho^2)s},
    \qquad s=\mu^\top\Sigma^{-1}\mu.
\end{equation}
The denominator is the contribution of label-mixture covariance. Omitting it gives the familiar noise-dominated approximation $\rho^2d$, which can be inaccurate when features are already strong. The result is an exact moment calculation under the stated assumptions; it is not a Bayes-error expression, a threshold theorem for a stochastic block model, or a performance guarantee for a trained GNN.

We then examine two empirical quantities as descriptions rather than theories. The first is the absolute accuracy difference $\mathrm{Acc}_{\mathrm{MLP}}-\mathrm{Acc}_{\mathrm{GNN}}$, which records observed aggregation damage but does not identify its cause. The second is two-hop label recovery, which can reveal longer-range class structure in a heterophilic graph. Audited paired results show that strong two-hop recovery does not guarantee that a multi-hop graph model beats a tuned MLP, while absent recovery does not imply that graph structure is useless. These failures are central evidence against using either statistic as an automatic selector.

For completeness, $1-\mathrm{Acc}_{\mathrm{MLP}}$ is retained only as arithmetic positive-gain headroom: no classifier's accuracy can exceed one. This quantity cannot bound negative GNN damage, does not measure conditional graph information, and shares an MLP term with the observed GNN--MLP gap. We therefore do not treat headroom correlations or non-violation counts as evidence of structural predictiveness.

The paper is organized around three contributions and evaluation targets:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Scoped aggregation discriminability analysis.} We derive exact fixed-degree ratios for neighbor-only averaging and a prescribed center--neighbor mixture while retaining label-mixture covariance, and state where these linear operators differ from practical GNN layers.
    \item \textbf{Direct validation target.} We define the required controlled comparison between empirical moment-based discriminability and the exact expression. Existing downstream accuracy sweeps are not presented as direct validation; the confirmatory parameter sweep is reported only after its frozen artifacts pass provenance checks.
    \item \textbf{Negative study of simple diagnostics.} Using audited paired results and matched scoring baselines, we show that aggregation damage and two-hop recovery are useful descriptions but insufficient model-selection rules. Historical aggregate-only results are treated as retrospective evidence rather than confirmatory validation.
\end{enumerate}

The remainder of the paper reviews related work, presents the motivating observations and scoped discriminability analysis, separates arithmetic headroom from structural prediction, evaluates the descriptive diagnostics and their failures, and concludes with the limits of the current evidence.
