\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We have studied when graph aggregation helps or harms node classification through SNR dynamics, aggregation damage, and heterophily recovery diagnostics. The results form a scoped diagnostic framework rather than a universal model-selection theory.

In the fixed-degree conditional-neighbor model, the exact ratio $\kappa=\rho^2d/[1+(1-\rho^2)s]$ identifies how edge correlation, degree, and feature strength jointly change one-hop Mahalanobis discriminability. This is a scoped mechanism calculation rather than a new broadcasting threshold or a guarantee for trained GNNs. ADR complements the calculation with an empirical measure of loss relative to feature-only prediction. The two-hop recovery ratio diagnoses one heterophily mechanism, but the audited failures show that it does not uniquely select a model family. Finally, $1-\mathrm{Acc}_{\mathrm{MLP}}$ is retained only as arithmetic accuracy headroom; the shared-term audit does not support treating its raw correlation as independent predictive evidence.

\subsection{Theoretical Significance and Scope}

The central theoretical contribution is the scoped SNR analysis, not a universal guarantee for trained GNNs. Finite graphs, learned transformations, dependent neighborhoods, and optimization can shift the predicted transition.

\subsection{Limitations and Future Work}

Our framework assumes: (1) binary or balanced multi-class problems, (2) homogeneous edge probabilities, (3) Gaussian or sub-Gaussian features. Extensions to heterogeneous graphs, imbalanced classes, and non-parametric feature distributions remain open.

Future directions include: adaptive architectures that estimate $\kappa$ online, theoretical analysis of attention mechanisms, and extension to heterogeneous and temporal graphs.
