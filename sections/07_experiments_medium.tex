\section{Experimental Validation}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Setup}

\textbf{Datasets.} We evaluate across citation, co-authorship, Wikipedia, WebKB, and OGB graphs. Table~\ref{tab:budget_validation} reports the audited subset for which the current artifact contains directly traceable results:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textit{Citation networks}: Cora, CiteSeer, PubMed (2.7K--20K nodes)
    \item \textit{Co-authorship}: Coauthor-CS, Coauthor-Physics (18K--35K nodes)
    \item \textit{Wikipedia}: Chameleon, Squirrel (2K--5K nodes)
    \item \textit{WebKB}: Texas, Wisconsin, Cornell (183--252 nodes)
    \item \textit{Large-scale OGB}: ogbn-arxiv (169K nodes)
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Models.} MLP (feature-only baseline), GCN \cite{kipf2017gcn}, GAT \cite{velivckovic2018gat}, GraphSAGE \cite{hamilton2017graphsage}, H2GCN \cite{zhu2020beyond}, LINKX \cite{lim2021large}, GPR-GNN \cite{chien2021gprgnn}.

\textbf{Protocol.} Unless noted otherwise, benchmark experiments use 10 random seeds and 60/20/20 train/validation/test splits. The audited ogbn-arxiv result uses the official split and 3 seeds. Several legacy aggregate files do not retain paired per-seed outputs. We report inferential tests only when their underlying samples are present in the audited artifact; otherwise results are treated as descriptive point estimates.

The check detects transcription or scale errors, but its absence of violations is not evidence that headroom predicts graph utility. Negative graph-model gaps are not bounded by this quantity.

\subsection{Structural Intervention: Legacy Edge Randomization}

The legacy experiment randomizes edges to perturb the observed topology. Its implementation intended to retain node degrees, but the audited artifact does not include a before/after degree sequence or checksum, so that property is not verified here. The intervention disrupts several graph statistics simultaneously and is therefore descriptive evidence of sensitivity to a topology perturbation rather than a standalone causal identification result.

\subsection{Retrospective Selector Audit}

Historical controlled, leave-one-dataset-out, and external summaries used different prediction targets, favorable tie or abstention scoring, and aggregate files that do not all retain paired seeds. We therefore do not pool their headline accuracies. In the audited six-dataset comparison with one target and matched scoring, the historical rule and an always-graph baseline make the same number of correct selections. This result demonstrates no incremental value for the rule in that comparison. A confirmatory selector evaluation requires a predeclared model set, tie margin, coverage policy, and per-seed outcomes.

\subsection{Two-Hop Diagnostic Audit}

Table~\ref{tab:2hop} reports the archived structural point estimates. The result file does not retain the class counts or resampled node pairs needed to reconstruct the class-prior agreement baseline or uncertainty for $h_1$, $h_2$, and $\Delta h$. We therefore do not attach significance language to the structural differences.

The paired model outcomes tell a different and useful negative story. H2GCN--MLP mean differences (95\% paired bootstrap intervals) are +6.3 points [1.3, 11.1] on Texas, $-0.8$ [$-4.3$, 2.5] on Wisconsin, +1.3 [$-2.6$, 5.0] on Cornell, +14.1 [12.2, 15.9] on Chameleon, and +10.0 [8.1, 11.8] on Squirrel. The ordering of these intervals does not follow the ordering of $R$ or $\Delta h$, so the archived evidence does not support a two-hop-only selector.

\subsection{Diagnostic Reporting Checklist}

The revised artifact uses the following reporting checklist rather than an automatic selector:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item report feature-only and graph-model accuracies on identical splits and seeds;
    \item report ADR as a paired percentage-point difference with uncertainty;
    \item report $h_0$, $h_1$, $h_2$, $\Delta h$, and any optional ratio with its denominator;
    \item mark unavailable provenance or uncertainty instead of imputing it; and
    \item evaluate any future decision rule prospectively against simple baselines with a predeclared target and tie policy.
\end{enumerate}
