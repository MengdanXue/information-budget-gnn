\IEEEtitleabstractindextext{
\begin{abstract}
Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) exhibit inconsistent performance across datasets: sometimes improving over feature-only methods (MLPs) by 20\%+, other times underperforming by similar margins. We develop a rigorous information-theoretic framework that explains this phenomenon through the \textbf{Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR)} dynamics of message passing.

Our central theoretical contribution is the \textbf{Aggregation SNR Ratio} theorem: under the sparse Contextual Stochastic Block Model (CSBM) with Gaussian features, one-hop mean aggregation changes the discriminability (squared Mahalanobis distance) by a factor $\kappa = \rho^2 k$, where $\rho = 2h-1$ is the edge correlation and $k$ is the average degree. This yields a sharp threshold $|\rho| = 1/\sqrt{k}$ (equivalently $h \in (0.34, 0.66)$ for $k=10$) below which aggregation \textit{reduces} classification performance. We establish quantitative error bounds: the independence violation contributes $O(k^4/n)$ via coupling arguments, cycle contamination adds $O((k-1)^{2t}/n)$ for $t$-layer propagation, and the multi-layer SNR follows $\mathcal{D}^{(t)} = ((k-1)\rho^2)^t \mathcal{D}^{(0)}$ connecting to the Kesten-Stigum threshold.

From this theoretical foundation, we derive three practical principles: (1) \textbf{The Information Budget}: GNN improvement is bounded by $(1 - \text{Acc}_{\text{MLP}})$, validated on 19 benchmarks (19/19) and 4 OGB datasets at million-node scale. (2) \textbf{Concatenation Preserves Information}: The Data Processing Inequality guarantees $I(Y; X, A) \geq I(Y; X)$, explaining GraphSAGE's substantially lower damage ratio ($\sim$189$\times$ in controlled settings, $\sim$20$\times$ on real data). (3) \textbf{Dual Heterophily Types}: 2-hop recovery ratio $R > 1.5$ distinguishes recoverable (H2GCN effective) from irrecoverable (MLP optimal) heterophily. Our framework achieves 88.9\% prediction accuracy (32/36) on CSBM experiments and 77.8\% (7/9) on external validation.
\end{abstract}
