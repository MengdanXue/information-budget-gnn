\begin{abstract}
Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) can either improve upon or underperform feature-only models, even on graphs with similar homophily. We study this variation through the signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) dynamics of aggregation, measured performance damage relative to an MLP, and two-hop recovery patterns in heterophilic graphs.

Our theoretical component is an exact fixed-degree calculation for one-hop mean aggregation. With edge correlation $\rho$, degree $d$, and feature strength $s=\mu^\top\Sigma^{-1}\mu$, the squared-Mahalanobis discriminability ratio is $\kappa=\rho^2d/[1+(1-\rho^2)s]$. The denominator retains the label-mixture covariance omitted by the common noise-dominated approximation $\rho^2d$. The transition therefore depends jointly on topology and feature strength. We relate the signal-squared-times-branching-factor form to classical broadcasting theory only as context, not as a new Kesten--Stigum threshold.

We complement the approximation with the \textbf{Aggregation Damage Ratio} (ADR), the accuracy loss relative to a feature-only baseline. In a controlled mid-homophily setting, the recorded loss is 18.9 percentage points for GCN and 0.1 points for GraphSAGE, supporting the role of feature-preserving concatenation without claiming a universal architecture ranking. For heterophilic graphs, we use the two-hop recovery ratio as a structural diagnostic; audited results show that it identifies a recovery mechanism but does not uniquely determine the best model. A frozen decision rule obtains 32/36 accuracy on controlled CSBM configurations and 7/9 on external datasets, while leave-one-dataset-out evaluation obtains 12/12 selective accuracy at 75\% coverage. We retain $(1-\mathrm{Acc}_{\mathrm{MLP}})$ only as an arithmetic accuracy-headroom check, not as a theoretical contribution or independent predictor.
\end{abstract}
